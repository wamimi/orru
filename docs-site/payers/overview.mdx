---
title: "Becoming a payer"
description: "How an employer, platform or grant programme makes their payments provable."
---

Orru turns payments you already make into income your workers can prove — without
you vouching for them, holding their data, or answering support tickets about it.

You are not asserting anything about a person. You are recording that you paid,
in a form a third party can verify against Ethereum without asking either of us.

## Two models

Which one you use depends on one thing: whether the payment itself is on a chain
Attestcoin can read.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="On-chain payment" icon="link">
    You pay in an ERC-20 on Ethereum. Attestcoin authenticates **the transfer
    itself**, inside the same receipt as your anchor.

    Strongest claim available. The amount is public, because the transfer is.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Anchored payment" icon="lock">
    You pay however you like — bank transfer, another chain, payroll provider —
    and anchor only a commitment on Ethereum.

    **The amount appears on no chain.** What is authenticated is your
    attestation.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
These prove different things and you should not blur them. On-chain proves money
moved. Anchored proves *you said* money moved, authenticated and timestamped.
The second is how payroll verification has always worked — an employer asserting
what they paid — but it rests on your identity, not on cryptography alone.
</Warning>

## What you commit to

<Steps>
  <Step title="Get approved once">
    An admin calls `setPayerApproval(yourAddress, true)` on the attestation
    registry. Until then your events are ignored — this is what stops someone
    deploying a lookalike contract and minting themselves an income history.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Anchor each payment run">
    One transaction per run, not per worker. Up to 32 commitments per batch.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deliver the slip, if you anchor">
    For anchored payments, the worker needs `{ amount, period, salt }` to build
    their proof. You are the only one who has it. On-chain payers skip this —
    the payroll contract publishes it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

That is the whole integration. No webhook to maintain, no data to store on our
behalf, no API key.

## What you never do

- **Vouch for a person.** You record a payment. Whether that qualifies someone
  for credit is the lender's decision.
- **Hold their credential.** It belongs to the worker's wallet and they authorize
  its issuance themselves.
- **See who verified it.** You do not learn that a worker applied for anything.
- **Pay for verification.** Reads are free and permissionless.

## The identity rule that matters

The payer is taken from `topics[1]` of the anchoring event — the `msg.sender`
that called the anchor — **never the transaction's `from`**.

<Note>
If you anchor through a relayer, a multisig, a smart account or an EIP-7702
delegation, the gas payer differs from you. Reading `from` would attribute your
workers' income to whoever paid gas. It is silent when it happens and it looks
like a registry bug. Orru reads `topics[1]`.
</Note>

## Cost

One anchor transaction per payroll run on Ethereum. Batched, so a run of thirty
workers is one transaction carrying thirty 32-byte hashes.

<Info>
Verification cost rises with event age — roughly tenfold after 24 hours. Anchor
promptly, and have whatever relays your anchors run at least daily.
</Info>
